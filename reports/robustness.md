# Robustness study

Generated by `make robustness` (`python -m gauges.robustness`) on 2026-10-15 from the scored history through 2026-10-15. Seed 20261015; 2,000 Monte Carlo paths for strategies, 10,000 for the 12-month fans; 1,000 bootstrap draws for intervals.

**How to read this.** Every reading is the gauge as it stood that day, computed from data public by then (the same point-in-time history the dashboard draws). Three things flatter or weaken it, and each table says when they matter:

- **Coverage.** G1's live weight is under 40% before 2007, so early readings rest on few inputs. History counts as reliable from 2007-01-03, when G1 coverage stays at or above 50%. G4 has almost no history before 2019 and is left out of the event study.
- **In-sample choices.** The Phase 3 backtest removed indicators using this same history (reports/validation.md), which flatters every result below to some degree. Rules here use the dashboard's band edges, not fitted thresholds; the grids show how much the choice matters.
- **Hindsight in the AI basket.** G3 and G4 use today's AI companies applied backwards, so their history is optimistic about which names mattered.

Returns are S&P 500 price returns for events and episodes, and SPY total return (dividends reinvested) for strategies, with idle cash earning the fed funds rate. Strategies act on the close after the signal.

## 2. Peak fear and peak greed

An episode is a run of days in the zone, with visits less than 21 trading days apart merged. Returns are measured from the first day in the zone (tradeable), the most extreme day (hindsight: you only know it afterwards) and the first day out (tradeable). _p_ is the share of 10× more random-entry draws (the same number of random days with a reading) whose average beat the episodes': small p after fear means better-than-random returns; small p after greed means worse-than-random.
